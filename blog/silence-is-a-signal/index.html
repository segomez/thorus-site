Silence is a signal: how to tell when a vendor relationship is drifting

If your consultant or software firm only reaches out when they need a signature, you are being managed toward the invoice.

Sebastián Gómez2 min readRelated situation: My digital transformation is going sideways

Your consultant only reaches out when they need you to sign something. That is a warning sign, not a workflow.

Healthy engagements have a rhythm. A weekly check-in. A short written update on Friday. A phone call when a decision is coming, before the paperwork lands in your inbox.

If the pattern at your company looks like silence, silence, silence, DocuSign, silence, you are being managed toward the invoice, not toward the outcome.

Three signals to watch this month

  • You don’t know what the team worked on last week. Ask for a written summary of the last two weeks. If it takes more than a day, nobody was tracking the work either.
  • Every update meeting ends with a request for more money or more time. A good partner brings you decisions, trade-offs and progress against the original plan, not a running list of asks.
  • The person from your first sales call has disappeared. You bought the senior partner. Three months in, you have a junior with no handoff document.

The fix is usually small

Stalled engagements often recover without changing the scope. What changes is the communication: a 30-minute Friday call, a one-page status template, and a named owner on each side. When both sides know who decides and what “progress” looks like, the relationship tends to recover within weeks.

Silence is not focus. Silence is a signal.
